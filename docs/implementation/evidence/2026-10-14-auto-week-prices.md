# Remove the waiting-for-prices workflow

The manager chose automatic reuse of prior prices with later editing. New weeks are created open immediately. Each active product receives its most recent positive saved price from an earlier week, skipping empty price rows and falling back to consumer price when a trader price is unavailable. The old copyPreviousPrices=false input is accepted for compatibility but no longer creates an unpriced week. New products without a prior price can have their price entered on the open week's settings; they do not put the entire week into a waiting state.

The web start-week, dashboard, orders and cycle list no longer present waiting-for-price or price-locking steps. The week settings always expose editable prices for open operational weeks. Changes apply to new orders; existing order line prices, bills and paid balances are preserved. The old open endpoint remains compatible for old clients and existing drafts. Calendar labels no longer advertise a waiting state.

Validation: 34 domain/contract/cycle/new-week cases and 8 order/payment/reader integration cases passed on isolated synthetic databases. Coverage includes immediate opening, prior consumer/trader prices, idempotency, editable open-week prices, stable existing paid bills and missing-product-price entry. Changed-file lint and web production build are checked before release.

Production preparation identified exactly two empty October draft weeks (5 and 12 October). Neither contained orders or payments. The current week already had eight positive price rows, which were preserved; the older week had eight empty rows filled from earlier saved prices. A private pre-change snapshot and rollback-only rehearsal verified these settings changes. Actual activation then completed with the same version/no-order assertions in one transaction: both weeks are open with eight positive product prices each, and zero existing orders changed. No September records or schema were changed.
